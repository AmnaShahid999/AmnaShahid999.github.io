Master's thesis — Happiness Across Intersections

Columbia University · General Social Survey data

This project asked whether the predictors of happiness are the same across demographic groups, or whether they diverge once income, education, and identity intersect. It set out to test that with unsupervised and supervised methods rather than assume the answer.

A full analytical pipeline: mean-imputed, VIF-checked preprocessing; unsupervised exploration with k-means clustering (validated by elbow and silhouette methods) and PCA; and class-balanced multinomial logistic regression and random forest models — including two- and three-way interaction terms — predicting self-reported happiness across demographic intersections.

QSLI — Quality of Student Learning Index

Habib University

Habib University had no single way to measure whether it was actually delivering a good education — quality lived in scattered spreadsheets and anecdotes. This project set out to build one shared, measurable definition of institutional quality that leadership could actually track.

Designed a five-index institutional quality framework — Student Learning, Academic Programs, Faculty Performance, Student Support Services, and Alumni Success — each broken into weighted components (Proficiency Gains, Curriculum Design, Teaching Effectiveness, Student Well-being, Career Trajectory, and others) with defined indicators, data sources, and collection tools mapped down to the course, program, school, and university level. Delivered as an Excel-based scoring workbook and an institutional briefing document for university leadership.

Course evaluation response-rate analysis

Habib University · Fall 2021 – Spring 2025

Response rates to course evaluations had been declining for years, and no one knew whether that reflected genuine disengagement or just noise. This analysis set out to find exactly which students, programs, and faculty types were driving the drop.

Analyzed four years of course-evaluation response data using logistic regression to isolate which student segments, programs, course levels, and faculty designations were most likely to disengage — controlling for semester, program, and course level to find that juniors were 26% and seniors nearly 50% less likely to respond than freshmen, and full-time faculty saw 21% higher participation than visiting/adjunct faculty. Findings pinpointed specific schools, programs, and course levels for targeted intervention.

Difference-in-differences evaluation

KP Sehat Sahulat Program · Econometrics term project, Group 1

Pakistan's Sehat Sahulat program was expanding fast, but expansion isn't evidence of impact — it could just as easily have coincided with health trends already underway. This evaluation set out to isolate what the program itself actually changed.

Led the modeling, data analysis, and robustness testing on this team project (co-authored with three classmates): a baseline regression linking household characteristics to infant mortality, then a difference-in-differences design using Khyber Pakhtunkhwa as treatment and Sindh/Punjab as controls. The DID estimate (−0.026, p<0.1) indicates the program reduced infant mortality by roughly 2.6 percentage points; robustness checks confirmed low multicollinearity (VIF 1.08) and addressed heteroskedasticity with robust standard errors.

Applicant enrollment segmentation

Institutional admissions data

An admissions office had years of applicant records but no way to tell which applicants were actually likely to enroll versus just apply. This project set out to turn that raw, messy export into segments the admissions team could act on.

Cleaned a raw admissions export with regex-based validation — filtering out placeholder names, test accounts, and internal staff emails — then feature-engineered a multi-stage funnel (application, testing, interview, offer, enrollment) and used k-means clustering on standardized applicant features to segment applicants by enrollment likelihood, surfacing which combinations of test participation, interview attendance, and geography most predicted whether an offer converted to enrollment.

Geospatial compliance dashboards

D.C. Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection — Columbia practicum

D.C.'s licensing department could see compliance issues in a spreadsheet but not where they clustered across the city. This practicum set out to make that geography visible so non-technical staff could act on it directly.

Joined a ward-boundary shapefile with pharmacy licensing records — entity names, license status, and location — and ward population data to map license density across Washington, D.C.'s eight wards. Data operations overhaul

EasyPaisa / Telenor Microfinance Bank

The auto loan process at EasyPaisa was slow and inconsistent, and the underlying customer data was too fragmented to diagnose why. This project set out to rebuild the data operations behind the process, not just patch the symptoms.

Restructured data and customer operations across more than 100,000 records using MySQL and Zendesk, and redesigned the end-to-end auto loan process — work that saved PKR 5 million and raised customer satisfaction to 87%.

Hospital readmissions analysis

Self-directed — BigQuery, Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset

Hospital readmissions are expensive and often preventable, but the claims-style data behind them is rarely analysis-ready. I built this pipeline to practice turning that raw data into readmission insight, end to end, on my own.

Built a SQL pipeline across 101,710 encounters — CASE-derived utilization, complexity, and risk categories, subqueries and CTEs, JOINs, and window functions feeding a validated analytical table and a Tableau dashboard on readmission by risk, utilization, and complexity.
